Sectors

We work where climate finance is actually flowing, and where Korean technology and operating experience are credible to global financial institutions.

Food security and agriculture

Horticulture, irrigation, cold chain, post-harvest systems and agricultural data.

Climate-resilient food systems are a stated priority in the country strategies of MDBs across South and Southeast Asia, and adaptation finance, historically the smaller half of climate finance, is where concessional money is most available.

Project types

  • Climate-resilient and smart horticulture with cold chain
  • Irrigation modernisation and water-efficient farming
  • Post-harvest storage and processing
  • Agricultural data, early warning and extension services

What makes it bankable

  • An offtake or price mechanism for the produce
  • An operating entity that aggregates farmers and runs the asset
  • A written request from the agriculture ministry or province
  • A pilot with measured yield and water data

Funding sources

MDB sovereign windows and the climate funds they administer; bilateral concessional lending; impact and agriculture-focused private equity for the operating company.

Where Korean capability fits

Controlled-environment horticulture, sensors and farm management systems, cold-chain equipment and the operating know-how behind them.

Illustrative model, not a specific project

Illustrative project model: a reservoir, irrigation to farmland, and a solar array that powers the pumps. Water in blue, power in amber.

Water and wastewater

Treatment, reuse, flood resilience and utility efficiency.

Water is where adaptation finance meets a utility business model: a tariff, an offtaker and a public counterparty. That makes it easier to structure than most adaptation projects and more demanding on the counterparty than power.

Project types

  • Municipal and industrial wastewater treatment
  • Water reuse for agriculture, industry and cooling
  • Flood resilience and urban drainage
  • Non-revenue water and energy efficiency in existing utilities

What makes it bankable

  • A creditworthy utility, or a sovereign or sub-sovereign standing behind it
  • A tariff path that covers operations and debt service
  • Currency of payment and who carries the exchange risk
  • Environmental and social work started early, because water touches land and communities

Funding sources

MDB sovereign loans and guarantees, public-private partnerships with MDB participation, climate funds for the adaptation component, and blended structures for reuse projects with an industrial customer.

Where Korean capability fits

Treatment process technology, membrane and reuse systems, plant operation, and smart-utility software for loss reduction.

Illustrative model, not a specific project

Illustrative project model: a treatment and reuse plant between the reservoir and its customers, with reuse water going to farmland.

AI data centres and energy

Compute campuses with dedicated clean power, storage and water-efficient cooling.

Compute demand is arriving in emerging markets faster than clean power and cooling water. Projects that solve power and water together, with contracted supply on both sides, are the ones private and development capital can finance.

Project types

  • Data-centre campuses with dedicated renewable generation and storage
  • Grid-interactive power and energy management for compute loads
  • Water-efficient cooling using treated wastewater or reuse water
  • Energy-optimisation systems for existing facilities

What makes it bankable

  • Contracted power and water supply for the life of the debt
  • An anchor tenant or offtake for the compute capacity
  • Land, permits and grid connection in hand
  • Water-use and energy-use standards met, with data

Funding sources

Infrastructure and private-equity funds for the campus; development finance institutions' private-sector windows for the power and water components; export credit for equipment; commercial banks at financial close.

Where Korean capability fits

Energy storage and AI-based energy management, power electronics, water treatment for cooling, and construction and operating experience.

Illustrative model, not a specific project

Illustrative project model: a data-centre campus with dedicated solar and storage through a substation, cooled with treated reuse water.
